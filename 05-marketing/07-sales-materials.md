**The Situation**

[2-3 paragraphs describing:]
- Who the client is (anonymized if needed) and what they do
- The specific problem or opportunity they faced
- Why they needed external engineering support
- Any constraints (timeline, technical debt, team structure)

*Example:*
A Series B SaaS company with 50,000+ active users needed to rebuild their customer-facing dashboard. The existing frontend was a monolithic jQuery application that was slow, difficult to maintain, and increasingly misaligned with the design team's vision. The engineering team was focused on backend infrastructure and needed a senior frontend specialist to own the rebuild.

The timeline was tight: 8 weeks to deliver a production-ready replacement without disrupting the existing user experience. The design team had Figma mockups ready, but previous engineering attempts had failed to match the design intent.

---

**The Approach**

[2-3 paragraphs describing:]
- The technical strategy and architectural decisions
- How design intent was preserved throughout engineering
- The collaboration model (tools, communication, workflow)
- Any innovative solutions to specific challenges

*Example:*
I started with a one-week discovery phase: auditing the existing codebase, mapping the current user flows, and conducting a detailed review of the Figma files with the design team. This upfront investment surfaced three critical architectural decisions that would have caused rework if missed.

I proposed a component-driven architecture using React and TypeScript, with a design token system that mapped directly to the Figma design tokens. This ensured that every spacing value, color, and typographic choice in the code matched the design source of truth.

The build ran in 2-week sprints with async daily updates and a weekly sync with the product and design leads. I delivered working features for review every 3-4 days, catching misalignments early and keeping the feedback loop tight.

## 3. Proposal Template Outline

*Use case: Sent after discovery call to formalize scope, approach, timeline, and investment.*

### Document Structure

**Format:** PDF or Notion page. Branded header. Clean, professional layout.
**Length:** 4-6 pages.

---

**Page 1: Cover**

- Flavio Fusuma wordmark
- "Project Proposal"
- [Client Company Name]
- [Date]
- "Prepared by Flavio Fusuma | flaviofusuma.com"

---

**Page 2: Executive Summary**

*Purpose: Give the busy decision-maker everything they need in one page.*

Sections:
- **The Opportunity** (2-3 sentences summarizing the client's need)
- **The Approach** (2-3 sentences on proposed solution)
- **The Investment** (total cost range or fixed price)
- **The Timeline** (start date, end date, key milestones)

---

**Page 3: Understanding Your Needs**

*Purpose: Demonstrate that you listened during discovery.*

Sections:
- **Project Background** (what the client shared about their situation)
- **Goals and Success Criteria** (what "done well" looks like, in the client's words)
- **Constraints** (timeline, budget, technical, team-related)
- **Assumptions** (what must be true for this proposal to hold)

---

**Page 4: Proposed Approach**

*Purpose: Show how you'll solve the problem.*

Sections:
- **Technical Strategy** (architecture, technology choices, rationale)
- **Design-Engineering Integration** (how design intent will be preserved)
- **Collaboration Model** (communication cadence, tools, review process)
- **Risk Mitigation** (how you'll handle scope changes, blockers, unknowns)

---

**Page 5: Timeline and Milestones**

*Purpose: Make the timeline concrete and accountable.*

| Phase | Duration | Deliverables | Review Point |
|-------|----------|-------------|--------------|
| Discovery and Setup | Week 1 | Architecture document, environment setup | Kickoff review |
| Sprint 1 | Weeks 2-3 | [Core feature set] | Mid-project review |
| Sprint 2 | Weeks 4-5 | [Secondary features, polish] | Pre-launch review |
| Launch and Handoff | Week 6 | Production deployment, documentation, handoff | Final review |

---

**Page 6: Investment and Terms**

*Purpose: Be transparent about cost.*

Sections:
- **Pricing** (fixed price for defined scope, or weekly/monthly rate for retainers)
- **What's Included** (engineering, code reviews, documentation, post-launch support window)
- **What's Not Included** (design, copywriting, hosting, third-party service costs)
- **Payment Schedule** (e.g., 30% upfront, 40% at midpoint, 30% on delivery)
- **Terms** (IP ownership, confidentiality, cancellation policy)
- **Next Steps** (how to accept and get started)

---

**Closing Line:**
"I'm looking forward to building something great with your team. If you have questions about this proposal, reply to this email or book a follow-up call at flaviofusuma.com/contact."
