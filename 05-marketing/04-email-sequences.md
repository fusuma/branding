### Email 2: The Differentiator (Sent Day 3)

**Subject line:** Most engineers stop at "it works"
**Preview text:** Here's where I keep going.

**Body:**

Hey [First Name],

There's a moment in every project where the code works. The feature loads. The tests pass. Most engineers call it done.

I don't.

That's when I start asking: Does the transition feel right? Is the loading state graceful or jarring? Does the hover feedback match the design intent? Is the component accessible by keyboard?

These details are invisible when they're done well. But users notice when they're missing. Products feel "off" without anyone being able to articulate why.

This is what I mean by design-informed engineering. It's not about being a designer. It's about treating design intent as a technical requirement -- one that's just as important as performance or security.

## Email Design Notes

### Visual Guidelines
- **Header:** Minimal. Flavio Fusuma wordmark in #1E3A5F on white, or reversed on deep blue. No heavy banner graphics.
- **Body:** Clean, single-column layout. Max width 600px. Default system fonts or a clean sans-serif (Inter, -apple-system).
- **Links:** Amber (#F59E0B) underlined text, or deep blue (#1E3A5F) text buttons.
- **CTA buttons:** Amber (#F59E0B) background, dark (#1E3A5F) text, rounded corners, generous padding. One primary CTA per email.
- **Footer:** Simple. Links to website, portfolio, and social profiles. Unsubscribe link. Physical address (required by CAN-SPAM).
- **Images:** Use sparingly. A portfolio screenshot or code snippet image when relevant. Always include alt text.

### Technical Setup
- **ESP recommendation:** ConvertKit (now Kit), Resend, or Loops -- all support clean templates and good deliverability.
- **Sending domain:** Use a subdomain (e.g., mail.flaviofusuma.com) with proper SPF, DKIM, and DMARC.
- **Personalization:** First name minimum. Segment by interest (A/B/C/D from welcome email 5) for future sends.
- **Tracking:** Open tracking, click tracking, UTM parameters on all links.
