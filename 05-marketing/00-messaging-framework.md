## Core Brand Message

**One-liner:**
Craft at the intersection of code and design.

**Elevator pitch (30 seconds):**
I'm Flavio Fusuma, a software engineer who builds digital products where technical excellence meets design thinking. I work with CTOs, product leads, and agencies who need more than code -- they need a partner who understands how engineering decisions shape user experience. The result is software that performs flawlessly and feels intentional in every detail.

**Extended narrative (60 seconds):**
Most engineering work treats design as a coat of paint applied after the fact. I take a different approach. Every architecture decision, every performance optimization, every API design choice is made with the end user in mind. I've spent my career at the intersection of code and design because I believe that's where the best digital products are built. Whether you're a CTO scaling a platform, a product lead shipping a new feature, or an agency delivering for a client, I bring the technical rigor you need with the design sensibility your users deserve. That's not a nice-to-have -- it's what separates products people tolerate from products people love.

## Content Pillars

### 1. Technical Excellence
Topics: Architecture decisions, performance optimization, code quality, testing strategies, tooling and workflow.

**Purpose:** Establish credibility with technical audiences. Show depth of engineering skill.

**Example angles:**
- "Why I refactored a 2,000-line component into 12 focused modules"
- "The performance audit that cut load time by 60%"
- "My testing philosophy: what to test, what not to, and why"

### 2. Design-Informed Engineering
Topics: Bridging design and dev, UI implementation details, animation and interaction, accessibility, design systems.

**Purpose:** Differentiate from engineers who treat design as an afterthought. Appeal to product and design-oriented audiences.

**Example angles:**
- "How I read a Figma file differently than most engineers"
- "The CSS trick that made our designer's vision actually work"
- "Building accessible components without sacrificing aesthetics"

### 3. Trusted Partnership
Topics: Communication practices, project management, client collaboration, transparency, process.

**Purpose:** Build trust with potential clients. Demonstrate professionalism and reliability.

**Example angles:**
- "What I send clients every Friday (and why they love it)"
- "How I scope projects to avoid surprises"
- "The conversation I have before every project kicks off"

### 4. Behind the Craft
Topics: Personal workflow, tools and setup, learning process, career reflections, industry observations.

**Purpose:** Humanize the brand. Create connection and relatability.

**Example angles:**
- "My desk setup and why every detail matters"
- "What I learned from my worst project"
- "The book that changed how I think about code"

### 5. Industry Perspective
Topics: Trends in web development, the future of design engineering, opinions on frameworks, hiring and team building.

**Purpose:** Position as a thought leader. Drive engagement and discussion.

**Example angles:**
- "The design engineer role is here to stay -- here's why"
- "Why your next hire should blur the line between design and dev"
- "Framework fatigue is real. Here's how I decide what to use."

## Messaging Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- **Do** lead with outcomes and value, not technical jargon
- **Do** use specific numbers and results when possible ("cut load time by 60%" not "improved performance")
- **Do** acknowledge the client's expertise and goals -- position as a partner, not a savior
- **Do** use "I" and "you" to keep messaging personal and direct
- **Do** reference real work, real decisions, real results
- **Do** maintain warmth even in technical content
- **Do** be opinionated -- having a clear perspective builds trust
- **Do** respect the reader's time -- be concise and purposeful
- **Do** use the brand colors (#1E3A5F deep blue, #F59E0B amber) consistently in visual assets
- **Do** end with a clear, low-pressure call to action

### Don't

- **Don't** use buzzwords without substance ("synergy," "leverage," "disrupt")
- **Don't** position as a generalist -- the brand is specifically about the code-design intersection
- **Don't** oversell or use superlatives ("the best," "world-class," "unparalleled")
- **Don't** be self-deprecating or hedging ("I think maybe I could help...")
- **Don't** talk down to any audience segment
- **Don't** use aggressive sales language ("Act now!" "Limited spots!")
- **Don't** ignore the design angle when talking about engineering, or vice versa
- **Don't** copy competitor positioning -- the intersection framing is the differentiator
- **Don't** use stock photography that feels generic -- visuals should feel as intentional as the code
- **Don't** send any communication without a clear purpose and next step

## Message Testing Framework

When evaluating any piece of messaging, ask:

1. **Is it clear?** Would someone outside tech understand the core idea?
2. **Is it confident?** Does it state a position without hedging?
3. **Is it warm?** Does it feel human and approachable?
4. **Is it intentional?** Does every word earn its place?
5. **Does it live at the intersection?** Does it connect code and design?
6. **Does it serve the audience?** Is it about their problem, not just your skill?

If the answer to any of these is no, revise before publishing.
