# Tone of Voice

## 1. Voice Principles

The Flavio Fusuma brand voice is consistent across all channels. While tone may shift depending on context, the underlying voice — the personality behind the words — remains the same.

### The Four Voice Dimensions

#### Clear, Not Simplistic
We explain complex topics in accessible language without dumbing them down. We respect our audience's intelligence while removing unnecessary barriers to understanding.

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| "This component uses a compound pattern to share state between its children without prop drilling." | "This uses some complicated React patterns you might not understand yet." |
| "The trade-off here is bundle size vs. runtime performance." | "It's really complex, but basically it makes things faster." |
| "Here's what this means for your project timeline." | "As per our previous discussion regarding the aforementioned deliverables..." |

#### Confident, Not Arrogant
We make clear recommendations and stand behind our expertise. We acknowledge what we don't know. We never position ourselves as the only solution.

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| "I'd recommend TypeScript here. Here's why." | "Anyone still using JavaScript in 2026 is behind." |
| "This approach has worked well in similar projects." | "This is the only right way to do it." |
| "I'm not the best fit for this particular need, but I can recommend someone." | "I could do it, but it's beneath my skill level." |

#### Warm, Not Casual
We're personable and approachable. We use conversational language without being unprofessional. We build rapport without forcing familiarity.

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| "Happy to walk through this together." | "lol yeah that bug is wild 😂" |
| "Great question — here's how I think about it." | "Per my last email..." |
| "Looking forward to collaborating on this." | "Can't wait to crush it, bro!" |

#### Intentional, Not Verbose
Every word earns its place. We're thorough when depth matters, concise when brevity serves better. We never pad content for the sake of length.

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| "Three things to consider:" | "There are several important factors that one should take into consideration when evaluating..." |
| "This won't scale past 10K users. Here's the alternative." | "While the current implementation is functional, there may be some potential scalability concerns that could arise..." |
| A focused 800-word blog post | A padded 2,500-word post saying the same thing |

## 2. Tone Matrix

Tone shifts by context while voice stays constant. Here's how:

| Context | Tone | Energy | Formality | Example |
|---------|------|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Website copy** | Confident, inviting | Medium-high | Professional-warm | "Software engineering with the precision of a craftsperson and the empathy of a designer." |
| **Blog posts** | Thoughtful, educational | Medium | Conversational-professional | "I ran into this pattern three times last month. Here's what I learned." |
| **Client proposals** | Assured, structured | Medium | Professional | "Based on the requirements discussed, I recommend the following approach..." |
| **Social media** | Engaging, authentic | High | Casual-professional | "Spent the morning refactoring a component library. Sometimes the best code is the code you delete." |
| **Code documentation** | Precise, helpful | Low-medium | Professional | "Handles authentication state across the application. See AuthProvider for configuration options." |
| **Email (client)** | Direct, warm | Medium | Professional-warm | "Quick update on this week's progress — we're ahead on the API work and I have a question about the dashboard layout." |
| **Email (community)** | Open, generous | Medium-high | Conversational | "Thanks for the issue report. You're right — I've pushed a fix. Let me know if it resolves it on your end." |
| **Conference talks** | Energetic, clear | High | Conversational | "Raise your hand if you've ever debugged a CSS grid issue at 2 AM. Yeah. Let's fix that." |
| **Error messages** | Calm, helpful | Low | Neutral-warm | "Something went wrong loading your projects. Try refreshing, or reach out if this keeps happening." |

## 3. Writing Guidelines

### Sentence Structure
- Lead with the most important information
- Prefer active voice over passive voice
- Keep sentences under 25 words when possible
- Use short paragraphs (2–4 sentences max)

### Word Choice
- Use specific, concrete words over vague, abstract ones
- Prefer common words over jargon (unless writing for a technical audience)
- Say "use" not "utilize," "help" not "facilitate," "start" not "commence"
- Technical terms are fine when they're the right tool — don't avoid them for the sake of simplicity

### Formatting
- Use headers to create scannable structure
- Use bullet points for lists of 3+ items
- Use code blocks for any code references, even inline
- Bold key terms on first use in long-form content

### Punctuation
- Use the Oxford comma (always)
- Use em dashes — like this — for parenthetical emphasis
- Minimize exclamation points (max one per piece of content)
- Use periods in bullet points only if they're complete sentences

## 4. Vocabulary

### Words We Use
| Word | Why |
|------|-----|
| Craft / Craftsmanship | Core to our identity; elevates engineering to an art |
| Build | Active, constructive, tangible |
| Partner | Implies collaboration, not transaction |
| Intentional | Signals thoughtfulness and purpose |
| Clean | Describes our code, design, and communication |
| Scalable | Practical, forward-thinking |
| Thoughtful | Captures our approach to every decision |

### Words We Avoid
| Word | Why | Alternative |
|------|-----|-------------|
| Guru / Ninja / Rockstar | Overused, unserious | Engineer, specialist, expert |
| Cutting-edge | Vague, hyperbolic | Modern, current, up-to-date |
| Leverage | Corporate jargon | Use, apply, build on |
| Synergy | Meaningless buzzword | Collaboration, alignment |
| Disrupt | Overused, aggressive | Improve, rethink, reimagine |
| Guarantee | Risky promise | Commit to, aim for, ensure |
| Cheap / Affordable | Devalues work | Cost-effective, efficient |
| Simple | Can trivialize complexity | Straightforward, clear, focused |

## 5. Channel-Specific Guidelines

### Website
- Hero copy: Max 12 words. Clear value proposition.
- Section headers: Action-oriented or benefit-driven
- Body copy: Short paragraphs, scannable structure
- CTAs: Specific and benefit-oriented ("See my work" not "Click here")

### LinkedIn
- Open with a hook (question, bold statement, or observation)
- Share genuine insights, not self-promotion disguised as content
- End with a question or invitation to discuss
- Max 1,300 characters for optimal engagement

### GitHub
- README: Clear, structured, with quick-start instructions
- PR descriptions: Context, approach, and test coverage
- Issue responses: Grateful, specific, and actionable
- Commit messages: Imperative mood, max 72 characters

### Email
- Subject lines: Specific and scannable (not "Quick question" or "Following up")
- Open with context, not pleasantries
- One clear ask or update per email
- Close with next steps, not open-ended sign-offs

## 6. Content Principles

1. **Teach, don't preach.** Share knowledge generously. Position yourself as a fellow learner, not an authority figure handing down wisdom.

2. **Show the work.** Don't just share conclusions — share the process, the trade-offs, and the reasoning. People trust transparency.

3. **Be useful first.** Every piece of content should leave the reader knowing something they didn't before or seeing something from a new angle.

4. **Earn attention.** Never assume people owe you their time. Make every word, every section, every post worth the read.

5. **Stay grounded.** Hype fades. Fundamentals endure. Write about things that will matter in five years, not five weeks.
