# Messaging Hierarchy

## 1. Tagline

**Primary Tagline:**
> Craft at the intersection of code and design.

**Alternative Taglines (for contextual use):**
- "Engineering with intention." — For technical audiences
- "Code that feels right." — For design-aware audiences
- "Built to last. Designed to delight." — For business audiences

### Tagline Usage
- The primary tagline appears alongside the logo in headers, email signatures, and social profiles
- Alternative taglines are used in campaign-specific contexts where the audience skew is clear
- Never modify, extend, or combine taglines

## 4. Messaging Pillars

The brand communicates through three core messaging pillars. Every piece of content should map to at least one pillar.

### Pillar 1: Technical Excellence

**Core message:** "Engineering isn't just about making things work. It's about making things work well — today, tomorrow, and at scale."

**Supporting messages:**
- Clean architecture isn't a luxury; it's a business decision
- Every line of code is reviewed through the lens of maintainability
- Performance, accessibility, and developer experience are engineered in from day one
- Production-grade means production-ready — tested, documented, and built to last

**Proof points:**
- Open-source contributions demonstrating code quality
- Technical blog posts diving deep into architecture decisions
- Portfolio projects showcasing clean, scalable implementations
- Client testimonials about code quality and reliability

**When to use:** Technical discussions, engineering blog posts, GitHub presence, developer-facing content, portfolio case studies

---

### Pillar 2: Design-Informed Engineering

**Core message:** "The best digital experiences happen when engineering and design speak the same language."

**Supporting messages:**
- Understanding design systems makes you a fundamentally better engineer
- Pixel-perfect implementation isn't obsessive — it's respectful of the design process
- Component architecture and design thinking share the same DNA
- The user doesn't see your code; they see the experience your code creates

**Proof points:**
- Design system implementations in portfolio
- Component library contributions
- Collaboration testimonials from designers
- Blog posts bridging engineering and design topics

**When to use:** Design-adjacent content, agency pitches, portfolio presentations, social media thought leadership, conference talks

---

### Pillar 3: Trusted Partnership

**Core message:** "Great software is built on great relationships — transparent, direct, and committed to shared success."

**Supporting messages:**
- No layers between you and the person building your product
- Proactive communication means you never have to chase for updates
- I succeed when your product succeeds — incentives are aligned
- Long-term thinking over short-term shortcuts

**Proof points:**
- Repeat client relationships
- Direct communication model (no PM intermediaries)
- Transparent process documentation
- Client testimonials about partnership quality

**When to use:** Proposals, client-facing communications, about page, testimonial framing, partnership discussions

## 6. Audience-Specific Messaging

### For CTOs / Engineering Managers
**Lead with:** Pillar 1 (Technical Excellence)
**Support with:** Pillar 3 (Partnership)

> "You need an engineer who writes code your team will want to maintain long after the engagement ends. I build with clean architecture, thorough testing, and documentation that actually gets read — because I know your next hire will inherit what I build."

### For Product Leads
**Lead with:** Pillar 2 (Design-Informed Engineering)
**Support with:** Pillar 1 (Technical Excellence)

> "Your designers created a beautiful system. I'll implement it with the precision it deserves — pixel-perfect components, thoughtful animations, and the kind of attention to detail that makes users trust your product."

### For Agency Partners
**Lead with:** Pillar 2 (Design-Informed Engineering)
**Support with:** Pillar 3 (Partnership)

> "I speak both languages. Your design team won't need to over-specify, and your project managers won't need to mediate between design intent and engineering output. I get it — because I've studied both sides."

### For the Tech Community
**Lead with:** Pillar 1 (Technical Excellence)
**Support with:** Pillar 2 (Design-Informed Engineering)

> "Here's what I've been thinking about, building, and learning. No hype, no hot takes — just honest explorations of how to build better software."

## 7. Call-to-Action Framework

### Primary CTAs
| CTA | Context | Urgency |
|-----|---------|---------|
| "Let's build something great" | Main website, proposals | Medium |
| "See the work" | Portfolio section, social bios | Low |
| "Start a conversation" | Contact page, email signatures | Medium |

### Secondary CTAs
| CTA | Context | Urgency |
|-----|---------|---------|
| "Read the case study" | Portfolio cards, social posts | Low |
| "Explore the code" | Open-source references, blog posts | Low |
| "Get in touch" | Footer, about page | Medium |
| "Subscribe for insights" | Blog, newsletter signup | Low |

### CTA Principles
- Always specific over generic ("See the work" not "Learn more")
- Match energy to context (website is confident; blog is inviting)
- One primary CTA per page/screen — reduce decision fatigue
- Never use "Click here" or "Submit"
